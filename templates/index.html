What the number means

TRC here measures nonfatal recordable injuries and illnesses per 100 full-time-equivalent workers — cases serious enough to log on OSHA Form 300 (medical treatment beyond first aid, lost or restricted work, loss of consciousness), per 200,000 hours worked. DART covers cases involving days away, restricted duty or transfer. Fatalities are shown separately. These are case rates, not the percentage of workers injured. Where it comes from and what it misses

Establishments with 20–249 employees in designated high-hazard industries and those with 250 or more employees that are required to keep injury records must submit their annual 300A summary to OSHA electronically; OSHA publishes it. It is self-reported, un-audited, and under-recording is a known problem, so a zero is not proof of safety. Inspections and citations are a separate record — how to pull that too.
